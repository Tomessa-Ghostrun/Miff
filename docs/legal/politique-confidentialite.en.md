Miff is an app that lets you know your loved ones are okay, without bothering them. To make that work, Miff processes some personal data, including information related to your location. This page explains, simply, what is collected, why, who can see it, how long it is kept, and how you stay in control.

## 1. The essentials in 10 points

1. **On our servers, Miff keeps your last known location and, if you publish a photo, the place where it was taken: nothing else.** This means the latitude, the longitude and the time of the measurement, saved on our servers (Firebase, Paris region). There is no history of your trips on our servers: each new location replaces the previous one, and the place of a photo disappears with it (24 hours). **On your phone only**, the app also notes, for 35 days at most, the moments when you entered or left a trusted place, to show you how your time is spread in your Profile. This log contains no location, address or place name, it is sent to nobody, and you can erase it whenever you want (see article 4).
2. **This is not live tracking.** Your phone itself watches the trusted places. Your location is saved at certain moments (for example when you open or leave the app, after a significant move, or when you arrive at a trusted place), and your loved ones see the last saved location, with the time.
3. **Sharing is your choice, Miff by Miff.** Nothing is saved without your explicit consent, and you can stop at any time.
4. **Only the members of the same Miff who also share can see your location, the place of your photo and its description**: to see other people's, you must share yours. The creator of the Miff has no more rights than the other members.
5. **Your location and your photo (with its description, if there is one) are erased**, along with the date of your last photo, when you stop sharing, leave the Miff, are removed from the Miff, or when the Miff or your account is deleted. **After 24 hours**, the app only shows your location hidden, with its date, and your photo can no longer be read; they nevertheless stay stored until they are replaced or erased (see article 7). The date of your last photo, however, stays recorded after the photo has expired, as long as your sharing with the Miff is active.
6. **Miff needs "Always" location** (in the background, even when the app is closed) to work: this is what lets it notice your arrivals and departures without you having to open the app. Notifications, on the other hand, are optional: if you accept them, Miff records your phone's notification identifier so it can let you know when a loved one gives news **or declares an "I'm in danger" alert** (see article 3). **Only you can trigger such an alert, with a deliberate gesture; it only notifies the Miff you choose. The alert itself does not contain your location; a single measurement of your location is attempted when it is sent, as usual (article 4).** **For each notification it sends to a loved one, our server keeps a technical trace for 30 days (the type of notification, the Miff, the identifier of the person concerned and the date: never a first name, photo, place or location), so that this loved one's notification centre is complete; they can erase it (article 3). This record is being put into service gradually: as long as our server does not write it, nothing is recorded.**
7. **Your photo is taken with the app's camera (never from your gallery), published with the place where it was taken, and visible to the members of your Miffs for 24 hours**, then it can no longer be read. You decide whether to publish it or not, and you can add a short optional description (140 characters at most) that you write yourself: it is seen by the same members as the photo and erased with it. Your loved ones who kept notifications turned on are told by a notification that shows only your first name (never the photo, nor its description, nor its place). **Miff also keeps, in your status, the date of your last photo, as long as your sharing is active, only to order the cards in the Feed** (the person who published a photo most recently comes first): it stays recorded after the photo is gone (24 hours). A member of your Miff can keep a copy of it (a screenshot, which also shows the description, or saving the photo on their phone, only if they choose to): we cannot erase that copy (see article 4).
8. **Usage statistics and crash reports are turned on when you click "Join"** (or "Continue with Google", or "Continue with Apple" on iPhone) at sign-up (there is no checkbox), and you can turn them off at any time in Settings, on iPhone as on Android, as a single choice. Before that click, nothing is sent. **Your location is never sent to Google Analytics or Crashlytics.**
9. **No advertising, no selling of data.**
10. **One account, one phone at a time**, and you can delete your account from the app and write to us to exercise your rights.

**How your agreement is collected at sign-up (Terms and statistics).**
- At sign-up, the button is called **"Join"**. Just above it, a sentence says: "By tapping “Join” or “Continue with Google”, you accept our Terms of Use and our Privacy Policy, as well as the sharing of your anonymous usage statistics and crash reports (you can change this at any time in the Settings)." **On iPhone**, it also quotes "Continue with Apple": "By tapping “Join”, “Continue with Apple” or “Continue with Google”, you accept…".
- **If you sign in with Google (or, on iPhone, with Apple) from the "Sign in" screen and do not have a Miff account yet**, your account is created before you could read that sentence. A screen then shows it to you right away (“By tapping “Continue”, you accept…”), with two buttons: **"Continue"** and **"Cancel"**. "Cancel" (or the phone's back button) **deletes the account that was just created**, keeping nothing and sending nothing to Google Analytics or Crashlytics.
- **There is no checkbox.** Your click counts both as acceptance of the Terms of Use and the Privacy Policy, and as agreement to the sharing of usage statistics and crash reports. These choices are therefore **given together, in a single gesture, and sending statistics and reports is turned on from the start**.
- **Before this click, nothing is sent** to Google Analytics or Crashlytics.
- **What is collected by default after the click**: the simple events we have chosen, the automatic events of the statistics service (for example the first opening of the app or the start of a session), the technical device data described in the table above, and crash reports. The events we have chosen never contain your position, a place, a Miff name, a secret code or free text. Google does, however, infer an **approximate geographic area** (country, region, sometimes city) from your phone's IP address, without the app accessing your position: this is not your precise position. The app calls them "anonymous": they contain neither your name nor your e-mail address, but they can be linked to a technical installation identifier, and we cannot promise they are anonymous in the strict sense.
- **How to withdraw**: in the app **Settings** (Settings > Privacy), **on iPhone as on Android**, you can turn off usage statistics and crash reports at any time. It is **a single choice for both** (not two separate switches). This changes nothing about your account or your use of Miff. **If you have no network when you withdraw your agreement**, sending stops right away on your phone and stays stopped even if you close the app; your choice is recorded in your account as soon as the network is back (see the "Proof of consent" line of the table: pending-withdrawal mark).
- **Effect of withdrawal**: sending stops from that moment. What has already been sent is not erased immediately: it is kept until the end of Google's retention period (see article 7).
- **Sharing your location is not covered by this click.** It requires a separate agreement, given Miff by Miff (see article 4).
**Camera and photo.** The first time you open "Share news", the app explains what the camera is used for, then your phone asks for permission. The camera is only active while that screen is open, and never in the background. The photo you take stays in the app's memory until you publish it or start over: Miff does not save it in your gallery or anywhere else on your phone, and never reads your gallery. It is published without its metadata (date, device, location). Miff does not analyse its content (no face recognition), nor that of your description. If you refuse access to the camera, everything else in Miff keeps working.

**Optional description of your photo.** Under the preview of your photo, a field lets you write, if you wish, a short description (140 characters at most). You can publish without writing anything. It is **free text, written by you**: Miff does not write it, does not suggest it and does not check it. **It may contain personal data** (a first name, a place, a situation, remarks about another person, and even sensitive information such as your health) **if you choose to write it there**: only put what you are comfortable showing your circle, and avoid private information about other people without their agreement. Until you publish, the description stays in the app's memory, like the photo, and it is forgotten if you start over or leave the screen. Once published, it is saved with the photo, in each Miff where you publish it, and it **follows the photo exactly**: the same people can see it (see article 5), for the same duration (see article 7). **Miff does not analyse your description and does not filter it automatically. It is never sent to Google Analytics or Crashlytics, never written in the app's technical logs and never put in a notification**; our notification server does not use it, copy it or keep it. A published description cannot be edited: to change it, publish a new photo, which replaces the old one with its description.

**Saving a loved one's photo (only if you choose to).** In the full-screen view of a loved one's photo, a "Save this photo" icon lets you keep a copy in your phone's gallery. **Miff never saves a photo on its own: this only happens if you tap that icon.** On iPhone, your phone then asks for permission to add photos to your library (add only: Miff never reads your photos or your library). On Android, no permission is needed (the photo is added to the "Miff" folder of your gallery; on older versions of Android, you choose where to save the file yourself). The saved file contains no metadata (no shooting date, no location, no place of the photo). Once saved, the photo is a file on your phone: Miff can neither find nor erase it, and the 24 hours no longer apply to it.

**Notifications.** They are optional and never block use of the app. There are three kinds.
- **Reminders for you** are **scheduled by your phone itself**, from information already in the app (for example your status or your places): they go through no Miff server. You can turn them off in the app's Settings (Settings > Notifications) and in your phone's settings. **This includes a reminder about your Miff**: if a loved one's status stays orange or red for a while, your phone (and only yours) may suggest you check in on them ("Your Miff has turned orange"). This reminder is calculated on your phone from the status you can already read; our servers send no notification for it, and it is not kept in the record described below.
- **The "{First name} gave news" notification** is sent to your loved ones **from our servers**, through Google's notification services (Firebase Cloud Messaging) and, on iPhone, Apple's. So that we can send you some, Miff records your phone's notification identifier (the "token": see the table above), **but only after you have read the explanation shown in the app and accepted notifications on your phone**. Before that, nothing is recorded.
- **The alert notification** is sent to your loved ones **from our servers**, like the previous one, when someone in your Miff declares an "I'm in danger" alert or ends it. It uses the same notification identifier (the "token"), recorded under the same conditions: only after you have read the explanation shown in the app and accepted notifications on your phone.

About this notification:
- **What it shows**: "{First name} gave news", then "See it in your Miff.". Nothing else: no photo, no description, no location, no place, no last name, no Miff name. A first name appears on your phone's lock screen; if you would rather it did not, you can hide notification previews in your phone's settings.
- **Who receives it**: the other members of the same Miff who share their location and kept this notification turned on. Never you, for your own publication, and never a member who stopped sharing, left the Miff or was removed.
- **What Miff does not notify**: today, our servers send your loved ones no notification when you arrive at or leave a place, when you enter a danger zone, or to signal a delay or an absence. **The only notification of this kind is the "I'm in danger" alert, which only you can trigger: Miff never triggers it for you** (there is no automatic detection of danger or falls). If that ever changes, we will update this policy first.
- **How to turn them off**: (1) the "When a loved one gives news" switch in Settings > Notifications (immediate effect: your token stays recorded, but none of these notifications is sent any more); (2) the general notifications switch in the app, which deletes your token; (3) your phone's settings: the app notices this the next time it is opened and then deletes your token. - **Not a guarantee**: a notification may arrive late or not arrive (phone switched off, battery saving, app force-stopped…). What your loved ones published can always be found by opening the Miff.

About the alert notification:
- **Availability**: the alert is being rolled out gradually. As long as it is not available in the version of the app you use, you cannot declare one, Miff records no alert from you, and what follows about sending an alert does not concern you.

When the person ends it: "{First name} ended their alert" and "{First name} said they're okay." Nothing else: no location, no place, no photo, no last name, no Miff name. **The first name and the word "alert" appear on your phone's lock screen**; if you would rather they did not, you can hide notification previews in your phone's settings.

## 4. Location and your position, explained

This is the most sensitive part, so here is everything.

**Why Miff asks for "Always" location (in the background, even when the app is closed).** To automatically tell your circle that you have arrived at a trusted place or left it, without you having to open the app. These same arrivals and departures are also used, on your phone only, to show you how your time is spread between your Miff's places (see below). **Miff needs this permission ("Always", with precise location) to work**: until it is granted, the app asks for it again and the permission screen stays displayed. You can withdraw it at any time in your phone settings; the app will then ask for it again. Withdrawing this permission has no consequence on your account, but Miff cannot work without it. Notifications are optional.

**How it works.**
- Miff uses the "region monitoring" features of iOS and Android: your phone itself detects when you enter or leave a trusted place. To choose which places to watch (the 20 closest to you, because of a limit on phones), it reads its position at certain moments and keeps a copy on the phone.
- Miff **does not track your position continuously** and **does not build a history of your trips**: it keeps no list of your locations on its servers. On your phone, it only keeps a simple log of your arrivals and departures (see "The time-breakdown log" below).
- When your sharing is on, your phone writes your status (for example "at home") and your **last known location** in each Miff you share with. The app writes there at certain moments: when opened, when closed, after a significant move, when arriving at a trusted place, and at the moment you declare an alert. Your loved ones therefore see the last **saved** location, with the time it was measured: it may be several hours old and is not your live position.
- When you publish a photo, your phone measures your location at that moment and attaches it to the photo: this is "the place of your photo", described below.

**Your last known location, in detail.**
- **What is saved**: the latitude, the longitude and the time of the measurement, nothing else. No history: each location replaces the previous one. The place of a photo is described separately, just below.
- **Who can see it**: only the members of the **same Miff** whose sharing is on, and **only if they also share theirs** (reciprocity). The creator of the Miff has no extra rights. People who are not in the Miff see nothing.
- **When it is erased**: when you stop sharing with that Miff, when you leave it, when the creator removes you, when the Miff is deleted, and when you delete your account. It is erased at the same time as your status and your photo, without depending on a delayed automatic process.
- **Display after 24 hours**: the app only shows a location older than 24 hours hidden, with its date always visible. Please note: "hidden" does not mean "erased". It stays stored on our servers until it is replaced or erased, in the cases above (see article 7).
- **Never anywhere else**: your location is never sent to Google Analytics or Crashlytics, nor written in the app's technical logs, nor in a sharing message.

**The place of your photo, in detail.**
- **What is saved**: the latitude, the longitude and the time of **its own measurement**, taken by your phone at the moment you take the photo (it is not necessarily your last known location, which may be older or more recent). A photo is **always published with its place**: there is no such thing as a photo without a location. This is a second copy of a position, attached to the image, which may differ from your last known location for 24 hours. There is still no history: one photo, so one photo place, per Miff you share with.
- **Who can see it**: only the members of the **same Miff** whose sharing is on, **and only if they also share their own location** (reciprocity). As a result, a member who does not share their location cannot open your photo. The creator of the Miff has no extra rights.
- **When it is erased**: with the photo. Unreadable after 24 hours, then deleted from our servers as soon as possible; also erased when you stop sharing with the Miff, leave it, are removed, or when the Miff or your account is deleted (see article 7: physical deletion at exactly the 24th hour is not guaranteed).
- **What we cannot verify**: our servers cannot check that the location or the time sent by a phone are true, or that the photo matches the place shown.
- **A copy is possible**: a member who sees your photo can take a screenshot of it (which also shows your description, if there is one), or save it on their phone with the "Save this photo" icon (only if they choose to; the saved file only contains the photo: neither the place of the photo nor its description). We cannot erase any of these copies, and the 24 hours do not apply to them. Only publish what you are comfortable showing your circle.
- **Never anywhere else**: like your last known location, the place of your photo is never sent to Google Analytics or Crashlytics, nor written in the app's technical logs.

**The time-breakdown log (on your phone only), in detail.**
- **What is noted**: when your phone notices that your situation relative to a Miff's places changes (you arrive in a trusted place, you enter a danger zone, you leave it), the app notes the Miff concerned, the type of situation, a technical identifier of the place, and the date and time. These are the same moments as your status. **No coordinates, no address, no place name, no photo, no identifier of a person.** The app does not ask for your location in addition: it reuses what the place monitoring already detects.
- **What it is for**: only to draw the "Your time breakdown" chart in your Profile. The calculation is done on your phone. Miff draws no statistics from it and sends neither the log nor the chart to Google Analytics or Crashlytics.
- **Who can see it**: **only you**, on your phone. Not your loved ones, not the creator of a Miff, not the Miff team, which has no access to it. Someone holding your unlocked phone would see the chart, as they would see the rest of the app: think of your lock code.
- **It never leaves your phone**: it is not sent to our servers and it is excluded from backups (iCloud, computer) and, on Android, from transfers from one phone to another. If you change phone, or reinstall the app, it starts again from zero.
- **How long**: 35 days at most, on a rolling basis (the oldest moments are erased as time goes by).
- **When it is erased**: when you sign out, when you delete your account, when you leave a Miff, are removed or the Miff is deleted (for that Miff), and **whenever you decide**: in Settings > Privacy, "Erase my time history". This changes nothing for your loved ones. **Please note**: when you stop sharing with a Miff, tracking stops, but that Miff's log stays on your phone for up to 35 days, unless you erase it yourself. There is no switch to stop it being kept: if you would rather not have it, you can erase it at any time.
- **An indication, not an exact measurement**: arrivals and departures are dated at the moment the app receives them, which may be late (battery saving, phone switched off, app stopped). The chart only shows percentages and does not replace what you know about your day.

**The "I'm in danger" alert and your location, in detail.**
- **A single measurement, at sending**: when you declare an alert, your phone tries to measure your location **once**, at that moment and, if the measurement succeeds within a few seconds, saves it as your last known location (it replaces the previous one, as usual, in every Miff where you share). There is **no repeated measurement** during the alert, **no history**, **no live tracking**. If the measurement does not succeed, the alert is sent anyway.
- **Nothing changes about who sees it**: only the members whose sharing is on **and who also share theirs** (reciprocity), as for any location. A loved one who does not share their location **is notified of your alert, but does not see where you are**.
- **No location in the alert or in the notification.**
- **No new agreement to give**: this measurement is of the same nature as those of your sharing (same data, same recipients, same duration), and asks for no new permission. Miff shows no permission window during the alert.
- **Miff passes your location to no emergency service.**

**Your consent.**
- Before any system permission request, Miff explains in the app what location is used for, including the fact that it works even when the app is closed.
- **This agreement is separate from your click on "Join"**: accepting the terms and statistics never shares your location.
- Sharing with a Miff only turns on if you decide so. The agreement is given **Miff by Miff**, and it is recorded with its date and the version of the text you saw. Nobody can turn it on for you.
- **Your location is only saved after you agree to a consent text of version 3 or a more recent one**, which states clearly that the members of your Miff see your last known location. **The current text is version 4 (`2026-11-v04`)**: it also says that the place of your photos goes with them, that there is no history of trips on our servers, and that the app keeps, on your phone only, a log of your arrivals and departures (35 days, no location or address, never sent). If you had accepted a version older than 3 (one that did not mention location), Miff asks for your agreement again before saving your location.
- **If you had accepted version 3**, your sharing goes on with no pause. That version did not mention the place of the photo: so the app shows you, when it opens, a "A clarification about what you share" card ("Got it" or "Later": it comes back at most 3 times, then stays available in Settings > Privacy), and the preview of each photo says that it is published with your location. **Please note**: until you tap "Got it", you can publish a photo with its place while your recorded agreement is the version 3 one, which did not mention that place. We write it as it is: it is a known and accepted limit (we do not block the publication of photos for these accounts).
- You can **withdraw your consent at any time**, Miff by Miff, in the app settings (stop sharing), or by removing the location permission in your phone settings. As soon as you stop sharing with a Miff, your status (with the date of your last photo), your photo (with its description and its place), your last location and your alert, if any, for that Miff are deleted. (The time-breakdown log for that Miff, which is on your phone only, is not deleted when you stop sharing: see above. You can erase it yourself at any time.)
- The time-breakdown log shares nothing with your Miff, so it has no separate agreement. It is, however, fed by the same place tracking, which only works if you share with a Miff and "Always" location is granted.
- **Please note**: because Miff does not work without "Always" location, there is no way to use the app without sharing your location, and a photo is always published with its place. If you do not wish to share your location, you can choose not to use Miff, or delete your account.

**No hidden tracking.** Miff is not made to follow someone without their knowledge. The person concerned must know and agree. And your account works on **one phone at a time only**, so that an old phone does not send your circle a status that no longer matches you. The time-breakdown log is only visible to you, and you can erase it whenever you want.

## 5. Who sees what?

The members of a Miff see:
- your first name, last name, profile photo (if you signed up with Google, the Google photo taken over, which you can change or remove before saving your profile and then at any time, even if you had never chosen a photo in Miff) and **phone number** (visible to the members of each of your Miffs, whether or not you share your status);
- the Miff's trusted places: name, **address**, position on the map, radius, type and category;
- **if you share**: your status (in a place, outside a place, in a danger zone, traveling…), the time, **your last known location with the time it was measured** (only if they also share their location), your photo if you published one in the last 24 hours, with **its description if you wrote one, the place where it was taken and the time of that measurement** (only if they also share their location: a member who does not share their location cannot open a photo, and therefore does not see its description), your battery level and the state of your location permission, **and the date of your last photo** (it is part of your status; the app does not display it as a date, it is used to place your card in the Feed: the person who published a photo most recently comes first, so the order of the cards shows who gave news last);
- **if you declare an alert** in that Miff: the fact that you declared an alert, its start time, and your card placed at the top of their Feed, with the buttons to call or write to you (your number is already visible to the members). **Even a member who does not share their location sees the alert**; they do not see your location (see article 4). **The members of your other Miffs see nothing.**
- if you do not share, that your sharing is turned off.

To see other people's status, location and photos, you must share yours. People who are not in the Miff see nothing. **A member can take a screenshot of what they see** (a photo, its description and its place, for example), **or save a photo on their phone with the "Save this photo" icon, only if they choose to**: we cannot prevent it, and "unreadable after 24 hours" applies to the app, not to a screenshot or a saved photo. The creator of the Miff has **no extra right to read** your status or your location. Anyone who knows a Miff's secret code can join it: only give it to people you trust, and be aware that its members see the address of the Miff's places.

The Miff team's access to the database is limited to authorised people and to what is necessary (maintenance, support, security).

**Your loved ones are told when you give news.** When you publish a photo, the other members of your Miff who share their location and kept notifications turned on receive a notification that contains your first name (see article 3). It contains neither the photo, nor its description, nor its place, nor a place name, nor your Miff's name. People who are not in the Miff receive nothing.

**Your loved ones are notified when you declare an alert, and when you end it.** The members of the Miff concerned who kept notifications turned on receive a notification that contains your first name and the word "alert" (see article 3). It contains no location, no place, and not your Miff's name. People who are not in that Miff receive nothing.

**Your time-breakdown log is only visible to you.** It stays on your phone (article 4): the members of your Miff see none of it and never see your chart.

**Your record of notifications is only visible to you.** The 30-day record of the notifications sent to your account (article 3) can only be read by you and only be written by our server: neither the other members of your Miff, nor its creator, nor the person who gave news or declared an alert have access to it. Those who receive a notification about you have, in their own record, a technical trace that contains your identifier (without your first name or photo: the app reads them from your member card when it is displayed).

We do not sell your data and we do not use it for advertising.

## 8. Your rights and how to exercise them

You have the right to:
- **access** your data;
- have inaccurate data **rectified** (you can edit your profile in the app; a photo description that is already published cannot be edited: publish a new photo, which replaces it);
- have your data **erased**;
- **receive** your data in a reusable format (portability);
- **object** to processing based on legitimate interest;
- ask for **restriction** of processing;
- **withdraw your consent** at any time (location, notifications, statistics, crash reports), without affecting what was done before;
- set **directives** on what happens to your data after your death (French law).

**How to do it:**
- **Delete your account**: directly in the app, in the settings. This deletes your account and the associated data described above. If you are the **creator** of a Miff, deleting your account also deletes that Miff, with its places, statuses, last known locations, alerts and photos. The other members then lose access to it: the app simply tells them, without any alarm, that the Miff no longer exists, and the data about them in that Miff, which has become unreadable, is erased the next time they open their app. Their own account is not affected and they can create or join another Miff. If you wish, let your loved ones know before deleting your account.
- **Stop sharing your location**: in the app settings, Miff by Miff. This deletes your status (with the date of your last photo), your photo with its description and its place, your last known location and your alert, if any, for that Miff at the same time.
- **Erase your time-breakdown log**: in Settings > Privacy, "Erase my time history" (immediate effect, no consequence for your loved ones). This log only exists on your phone: we have no copy, so there is nothing for us to send you or to erase on our side.
- **Erase your record of notifications**: in the app's notification centre, "Delete this notification", or "Clear all", also deletes the matching line of the 30-day record kept on our servers (article 3). Deleting your account also erases it (article 7).
- **Notifications**: in the app's Settings > Notifications ("When a loved one gives news" switch, general switch) or in your phone's settings. Turning off the general switch, signing out or deleting your account deletes your token (article 7). For alerts, only the general switch and your phone's settings apply (the "When a loved one gives news" switch does not concern them). **Ending your own alert**: at any time, by holding the alert banner of the app for 5 seconds.
- **Statistics and crash reports**: a single switch in the app Settings (Settings > Privacy), on iPhone as on Android (the two are linked). Turning it off stops sending from that moment, even without a network (see article 3); what has already been sent is kept according to the periods in article 7.
- **Access or export your data**: during the test phase (beta), write to us at **tomessa.mfouka@gmail.com**. We reply with your data in a readable format.

## 9. Security

We protect your data with appropriate measures: strict access rules (only members of a Miff can access that Miff's information; a member's last location and the place of their photo can only be read by the members of the same Miff whose sharing is on and who also share theirs), encrypted connections, encryption at rest provided by Google, verification that requests come from the genuine app (App Check), and access to administration tools limited to authorised people. ## 10. Minors

Miff does not impose a minimum age and does not ask for your age. Miff is designed for circles of loved ones (family, friends, partners), which may include young people. In France, the law provides that **below the age of 15**, the agreement of a parent or holder of parental authority is required for certain online services relying on consent. Miff cannot verify its users' age.

If you are a minor, we advise you to use Miff with your parents' agreement. If you are a parent and think that a child's data was collected without your agreement, write to **tomessa.mfouka@gmail.com**: we will delete their account and data.

Each member gives their own consent to share their location, and can stop at any time. This also applies to a minor: if they use Miff and share, their last known location, their photos, the descriptions they may have written and the place where they were taken are visible to the other members of their Miff, like those of any member, and those members receive a notification with their first name when they publish a photo (and our server keeps the 30-day technical trace described in article 3, which contains their identifier, without first name or photo). The date of their last photo, recorded in their status, follows the same rules as for any member. Miff does not analyse photos or descriptions and does not check their content or the age of the people in them. A member can also keep a copy (a screenshot, or saving the photo on their phone, only if they choose to): that copy escapes the 24 hours and we cannot erase it. The "I'm in danger" alert can also be triggered by a minor: it notifies the members of their Miff, as described in article 3, and those members receive a notification with their first name (with the same 30-day technical trace in their record). Miff checks neither the person's age nor their parents' agreement; a parent who is not a member of the Miff is not notified.

**Please note:** Miff does not verify its users' age. We therefore cannot check whether a parent's agreement was given where the law requires it.
