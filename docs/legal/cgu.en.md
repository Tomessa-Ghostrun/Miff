## Read this first: Miff is not an emergency service

> **Miff is not an emergency service, not a life-safety service, and not a medical monitoring device.**
>
> - Miff helps you reassure your loved ones in everyday life. A "all is well" status **is not a guarantee** that the person is okay. A late status does **not necessarily** mean something is wrong: a phone that is off, out of battery, without network, in power-saving mode, or with location permission turned off is enough to explain a delay.
> - Detection of arrivals and departures depends on your phone and its system. It can be delayed, imprecise, or may not happen. Notifications can also arrive late, or not arrive.
> - A location shown in Miff is the last **saved** location, not the live position: it may be several hours old. Do not use it to find someone in case of danger. Likewise, the place of a photo is where it was taken, not the person's current position.
> - **The "I'm in danger" alert, when available, notifies your loved ones; it does not notify the emergency services.** It is a notification between the members of a Miff: it can arrive late, not arrive (notifications turned off, phone switched off, no network, battery saving…), or not be read. Nothing guarantees that a loved one sees it, or that they can help you. If you are in immediate danger, call the emergency services first.
> - **If someone is in danger, or if you have a serious doubt about their safety, do not wait for Miff: call the emergency services. In France and across the European Union, 112 works everywhere (and in France: 15 for medical emergencies, 17 for the police, 18 for the fire brigade).**
>
> Miff is here to ease everyday worries. It replaces neither conversation between loved ones nor the emergency services.

## 2. What is Miff for?

Miff is an app to **know your loved ones are okay, without bothering them**.
- You create or join a **"Miff"**: your circle of trust (family, friends, partner…). You join a Miff with a **secret code**.
- The creator of the Miff defines **trusted places** (home, school, work…), with their address, visible to all the members.
- If you have agreed, your phone detects when you arrive at or leave a trusted place, and your circle sees an automatic **status** and your **last known location**, without you having to do anything. You can also **share news** by hand by publishing a photo, taken with the app's camera, to which you can add a short optional description (140 characters at most); it is published with the place where it was taken.

Your loved ones then simply see "sharing turned off", without any alert message.
- **What your loved ones see.** If you share, the members of your Miff see your status (for example "at home") and **your last known location, with the time**. **On its servers, Miff only keeps that last location** (latitude, longitude, time of the measurement) **and, if you publish a photo, the place where it was taken for 24 hours**, **with no history of trips**: each new location replaces the previous one. This is not live tracking. On your phone only, the app also keeps a simple log of your arrivals and departures to show you how your time is spread: only you see it, it contains no location and it does not leave your phone (see the Privacy Policy).
- **Your photo and its place.** If you publish a photo, it is **always published with the place where it was taken**, measured by your phone at that moment (with the time of that measurement). This place stays attached to the photo for 24 hours, even if your last known location changes in the meantime, and it does not tell where you are now. If you add a description to your photo (see article 8), it is published with the photo, to the same people and for the same duration.
- **Who sees it**: only the members of the same Miff whose sharing is on, and only if they also share theirs. This also applies to the place of your photo: a member who does not share their location cannot open your photo. The creator of the Miff has no extra rights.
- **Erasure**: your location, your photo (with its description) and its place, along with the date of your last photo, are erased when you stop sharing, leave the Miff, are removed from the Miff, or when the Miff or your account is deleted. After 24 hours, your location is only shown hidden, with its date, and your photo can no longer be read (they stay stored until they are replaced or erased); the date of your last photo, however, stays recorded as long as your sharing is active: it is only used to order the cards in the Feed. Details are in the Privacy Policy.
- **Reciprocity**: to see other people's status and location, you must share yours.

You can withdraw it at any time in your phone settings: this has no effect on your account, but Miff stops working until it is restored. Notifications, on the other hand, are optional.
- **Telling your loved ones when you give news.** When you publish a photo, the other members of your Miff who share their location and kept notifications turned on receive a quiet notification "{First name} gave news". They may arrive late or not arrive: this is not an emergency service. **For each notification sent to a loved one, our server keeps a 30-day technical trace** (the type of notification, the Miff, the identifier of the person concerned and the date: never a first name, photo, place or location), which lets their notification centre be complete; they can erase it. The details are in the Privacy Policy.

Today, Miff sends no notification about it. **A danger zone is not an alert**: the "I'm in danger" alert is a gesture only you can trigger (article 6 bis).

## 6 bis. The "I'm in danger" alert

- **Availability.** The alert is being rolled out gradually: as long as it is not available in the version of the app you use, you cannot declare one, and what follows about sending an alert does not concern you. A loved one whose app is not up to date may not see or not receive your alert.
- **What it is.** A deliberate gesture: by holding the button of the "Share news" screen for 5 seconds, you notify the members of the Miff shown in your Feed at that moment. They receive a notification, see your card at the top of their Feed and can call or write to you. It is **free**.
- **A single Miff.** The alert notifies **only** the Miff shown when you trigger it; its members are notified even if they do not share their location. Your other Miffs see nothing. Check the Miff's name before sending. To declare an alert in a Miff, your sharing must be on there.
- **What it does not do.** It **does not call or notify the emergency services**, the police, or anyone other than the members of that Miff. It detects no danger by itself: only you trigger it. It guarantees neither that a loved one receives it, nor that they read it, nor any delay, nor that they can help you. **If you are in immediate danger, call the emergency services first** (112 in France and across the European Union). The screen that follows sending offers you emergency numbers and a call to a loved one, through your phone's Phone app: Miff never calls on your behalf.
- **Your location.** When you declare the alert, your phone tries to measure your location once and, if the measurement succeeds, saves it as your last known location, as usual. It is **not** in the alert. Your loved ones see it only if they also share theirs (article 6).
- **How long.** The alert lasts **7 days at most**. Only you can end it, at any time, by holding the alert banner for 5 seconds; your loved ones are then told by a calm notification. After 7 days, it ends by itself, **with no notification**. It is deleted right away if you stop sharing with the Miff, leave it, are removed from it or delete your account; in these cases, no end notification is sent.
- **A cap, to protect the service.** You can declare up to **3 alerts in any 24-hour period**, all your Miffs combined. Beyond that, the app tells you so calmly and keeps emergency numbers available; you will be able to declare an alert again once the oldest one is 24 hours old. Ending an alert does not give that credit back.
- **If you receive an alert.** An alert is information that your loved one chooses to give you. Check in with them; if you have a serious doubt about their safety, call the emergency services. Miff does not tell you where the person is unless you also share your location, and does not tell you whether they are okay.
- **The details** (data recorded, durations, notifications) are in the [Privacy Policy](politique-confidentialite.en.md).
